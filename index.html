VGN: Value-Guided Navigation from RGB for Robot Motion Planning

Video BibTeX

Abstract

Visual navigation from monocular RGB requires reliable scene understanding and safe goal-directed decision making. VGN learns dense traversability and a goal-conditioned cost-to-go (CTG) representation directly from a single RGB observation. Instead of requiring trajectory-centric supervision or robot interaction, a teacher pipeline combines vision foundation models with classical planning to convert static RGB images into dense navigation-oriented pseudo-supervision.

At deployment, the teacher is removed. The student predicts traversability and CTG from RGB, providing an explicit planning representation that can be consumed by multiple downstream planners, including graph search, direct gradient rollout, and gradient-guided sampling.

Approach

Motivation

Most visual navigation methods either predict actions or trajectories directly, or rely on hand-designed intermediate costs. VGN instead learns a dense navigation representation from RGB that separates scene understanding from the downstream planning mechanism. The goal is to preserve explicit planning structure while retaining the flexibility of learned perception.

VGN

A teacher pipeline grounds semantic regions in the input image, converts these regions into a dense traversability map, samples a projected goal, and computes a traversability-aware CTG field using classical planning. A student network then learns to predict both traversability and goal-conditioned CTG from RGB. At test time, the same representation can support graph search, direct CTG-gradient rollout, or gradient-guided sampling.

Dataset

We train on RUGD and evaluate zero-shot on RELLIS, GOOSE, NaviTrace, and Cityscapes. The teacher generates dense traversability and CTG supervision from static RGB images, allowing the student to learn goal-conditioned navigation structure without expert trajectories. We also evaluate closed-loop navigation in Forest and City simulation environments and deploy the learned system on a Unitree Go2 for real-world testing.
