Abstract

Visual navigation from monocular RGB requires reliable scene understanding and safe goal-directed decision making. VGN learns a dense traversability map and a goal-conditioned cost-to-go (CTG) representation directly from a single RGB observation. Instead of learning only from expert trajectories or robot interaction, a teacher pipeline combines vision foundation models with classical planning to convert static RGB images into dense navigation-oriented pseudo-supervision.

The learned representation is designed as an interface to downstream planning rather than as a single fixed trajectory generator. At deployment, the same predicted traversability and CTG fields can support graph search, direct gradient rollout, or gradient-guided sampling for local image-space navigation.

During training, GroundedSAM-derived terrain regions produce dense traversability supervision. Dijkstra propagation over this field produces goal-conditioned CTG pseudo-labels. A student network learns both representations from RGB and runs without the teacher at deployment.

PLANNER-AGNOSTIC BY DESIGN

One representation, multiple planners

VGN does not directly commit to one trajectory decoder. The learned traversability + CTG pair is a reusable planning interface.
